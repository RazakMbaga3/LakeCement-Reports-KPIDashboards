## 1. Launch announcement — all staff

> **Subject: New Reporting System goes live [LAUNCH DATE]**
>
> Colleagues,
>
> From **[LAUNCH DATE]**, Nyati Cement's daily plant reports move to our new
> Reporting and Management Information System.
>
> **What it is**
> One place to record the daily reports each department already produces —
> production, power, stoppages, dispatch, materials, quality and the rest.
> Enter your figures once, and the system builds the KPIs and the management
> reports from them automatically.
>
> **Why we are doing it**
> Today the same figures are typed several times over, into different
> spreadsheets, and the monthly reports are assembled by hand. That costs
> time and it introduces errors. With one system, a figure is entered once
> by the person who knows it, approved by their head, and everyone
> downstream sees the same number.
>
> **What changes for you**
> If you fill in a daily report today, you will fill in the same report in
> the system instead. The figures are the same; the place you type them
> changes. Your department head reviews and approves as they do now.
>
> If you do not fill in reports, nothing changes for you day to day.
>
> **Who must use it**
> All staff who record or approve daily reports in: **[DEPARTMENT LIST]**.
>
> **Where to find it**
> **https://smr.lakecement.co.tz** — from any computer on the plant network.
> You will receive your username separately. You will be asked to set your
> own password the first time you log in.
>
> **Training**
> Sessions for your department: **[TRAINING DATE/TIME/VENUE]**. They take
> under an hour and cover your own report, not the whole system. Please
> attend — it is much quicker than working it out alone.
>
> **Getting help**
> **[SUPPORT CONTACT]**. If a figure ever looks wrong, please tell us
> straight away rather than working around it. Getting the numbers right
> matters more to us than the system looking busy.
>
> **One thing to remember**
> Saving a draft is not the same as submitting. A draft keeps your work, but
> until you press **Submit** the report has not been filed. Always Submit
> when you have finished.
>
> [SENDER NAME / TITLE]

## 2. Manager and HOD announcement

> **Subject: Reporting System — what it means for department heads**
>
> Colleagues,
>
> From **[LAUNCH DATE]** your department's daily reports come to you for
> approval in the new Reporting System rather than on paper or by email.
>
> **What you gain**
>
> - **Visibility.** You can see at a glance which days have been filed and
>   which have not, instead of finding out at month end.
> - **KPIs that build themselves.** Once you approve a report, the figures
>   flow into your department's KPIs, the analytics and the management pack
>   with no re-keying.
> - **A record you can rely on.** The system keeps what every report said
>   before any change, so "what did we report on the 14th" always has an
>   answer.
> - **Corrections without a developer.** An approved report is locked, but
>   your team can request a correction and you can grant a time-limited
>   window. Nobody edits figures behind the scenes.
>
> **What you are accountable for**
>
> When you approve a report you are certifying those figures. They go
> straight into what management reads. Two things follow:
>
> - Approve promptly. A report waiting in your queue is **not** counted in
>   any KPI.
> - Watch for reports left as **drafts**. A draft is saved but not filed —
>   the figures exist and count for nothing. Your team may believe they have
>   reported when they have not, and you are the only person who will see it.
>
> **Something to be straight about**
>
> Several KPIs will read **"no data"** at launch. That is accurate, not
> broken: five reporting streams — power consumption, stoppages, materials,
> limestone and quality — have had no submissions since June. The system
> deliberately shows "no data" rather than a zero, because a zero on an
> efficiency KPI reads as perfect performance.
>
> As your department resumes daily filing, those figures fill in. If your
> department is one of the five, restoring daily filing is the most valuable
> thing you can do.
>
> **Your training session:** [TRAINING DATE/TIME/VENUE] — about an hour,
> covering approvals, monthly KPI submission, and reading the dashboards.
>
> [SENDER NAME / TITLE]

## 3. Senior management briefing

> **Subject: Management information from [LAUNCH DATE]**
>
> From **[LAUNCH DATE]** plant reporting is consolidated into a single
> system, with management dashboards drawn directly from the daily reports
> departments file.
>
> **What this gives management**
>
> - **One version of each number.** Departmental figures, KPIs and the MIS
>   pack all derive from the same approved reports.
> - **Accountability by design.** Every figure is attributable: who filed
>   it, who approved it, and what it said before any change. Whoever reports
>   a figure is never the person who signs it off.
> - **Target versus actual** by department and month, with trends.
> - **Exports** into Excel for board and meeting packs.
>
> **Two things to know before relying on it**
>
> 1. **Coverage is incomplete today.** For August and September, 21 of the
>    32 KPIs read "no data", because five reporting streams stopped being
>    filed after June. This is a reporting-discipline gap, not a system
>    fault, and it is the main focus of the launch. The system shows "no
>    data" rather than a zero precisely so that a gap cannot be mistaken for
>    good performance.
>
> 2. **Some KPIs are flagged "provisional".** Where a figure has a known
>    limitation, the system says so alongside it in plain language. Those
>    notes should be read before a figure is quoted externally.
>
> We would rather present an incomplete picture honestly than a complete
> picture that is partly invented.
>
> **What management involvement is needed**
>
> - Reinforcing that daily reporting is expected, and by when
> - A ruling on two open definition questions, listed separately
> - Confirming who receives system alerts and the daily summary
>

## 7. Tone guidance

For whoever adapts these drafts:

- **Plain language.** No "leverage", no "solution", no "onboarding".
- **Say what changes for the reader**, not what the system does.
- **Be honest about gaps.** The "no data" position is stated openly in every
  draft above. Staff will find it within a day anyway, and finding it after
  being told everything works costs more trust than the gap itself.
- **Draft vs Submit gets repeated in every message to users.** It is the one
  misunderstanding that silently loses a day's reporting.
- **Invite problem reports, warmly and repeatedly.** The failure mode for a
  plant MIS is not complaints — it is silence, workarounds, and a parallel
  spreadsheet nobody mentions.
